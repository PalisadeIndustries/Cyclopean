Game Overview

The veil has been pulled back, the ignorance of mankind blasted away in an instant. In the aftermath of the great revelations brought on by waking gods, mankind has adapted. Now the cultic armies of mankind clash in fierce territorial battles to carve out empires from what is left of the world. Infantry advance under the watch of priests and sorcerers, tanks provide fire support for eldritch abominations, and technoccultists combine technology and the arcane into strange and twisted technologies. Mankind fights alongside monsters and harnesses magic to curry favor from their new masters in the age Cyclopean.

This is not a broken world. Mankind has been given a new vision for the future. As cults carve out territory, stability is valued, and some semblance of normality returns. The new strange, the new normal, has overcome the world, and with it a new type of war.

Cyclopean is a fast-paced 15mm tabletop miniature wargame for two or more players set in a new epoch. Players take control of combined arms forces of soldiers, vehicles, aircraft, eldritch monstrosities, towering abominations, and conniving sorcerers. 

As their general, you harness and extract energy from your units to drive them onward. Men and beasts are strong, but this strange time has proven that they are stronger together. A mighty mech is nothing without the soldiers alongside it. A force must balance its composition so that energy flows to empower the right tool at the right time. 
Be hardy. You fight monsters: slay many.
— Brigadier General Chad Vance † Battle of Ellis Island

General Guidelines

Cyclopean is intended for rules to be written in plain English but some rules have more technical turns. There are some general guidelines across all rules that can be followed. If these rules are broken by a specific ability they will be noted as exceptions on that ability.

The standard working element for Cyclopean is the unit. A unit is made of up of a number of individual models, interchangeably referred to as stands. Stands is usually a reference to infantry while models applies to larger creatures and vehicles. A unit's card will specify how many models or stands a unit is composed of.

All models and units in your army are friendlies.

All models controlled by your opponent are enemies.

Abilities apply to the specific unit activating them and last until the end of the turn. If they affect another unit or have a different duration this will be specified.

Unless specified, abilities are assumed to only be able to target friendlies.

Some abilities will specify a type of unit affected. It will say "All X units" or "Select an X unit within range." Any unit that meets that criteria may be selected. A unit may even target itself with an ability if it is otherwise eligible and is always considered within range of itself.

Abilities with the same name are not cumulative.

Abilities with different names are cumulative.



Attachments count as the unit they belong to for all rules affecting them and vice-versa. If an attachment's ability specifies 'this unit' then it applies to the parent unit as well and any bonuses from the parent unit apply to the attachment.

Dice pools may be modified. They will be specified if they modify the pool as a whole or modify based on the number of stands. This is the number of stands still active in the unit. 

Some units Transform. In this case, each eligible stand is replaced by the appropriate stand of the unit it transformed into. Try to maintain as accurate of a location as possible when swapping miniatures.

Some units may be Summoned. A summoned unit is placed within 6" of the unit creating it, or summoner. This unit is considered to have been activated already and does not contribute any Major or Minor energy.
Summoned Attachments

If the unit being Summoned is an attachment it may be added to an appropriate unit if it is within range. This unit does not contribute any energy this turn but may perform actions with its parent unit. 

Dice

Cyclopean is played with three different dice: the six-sided dice called d6, eight-sided dice called d8, and ten-sided dice called d10. These are used to determine hits and for resolving certain abilities.

Some abilities use a D3. To resolve this roll a D6 and divide the result by 2, rounding up. 

Whenever an attack is declared and a Dice Pool is formed, the Dice Pool is the dice from the same weapon added together and rolled as one. Successes from one dice pool may be combined to meet Wound Thresholds but the results of two separate dice pools may not be combined.

Common Rules


  • Impact X: This reduces the target's wound threshold when resolving attacks.
  • Anti-(Tank/Infantry) X: Each successful hit counts as X successes. One hit, however, cannot cause multiple wounds and all excess is lost.
Rules In Play

A unit of marksmen shoots at a unit of Tidewatch Pacifiers with their Anti-Infantry 3 weapons. They score 3 successful hits. 


When comparing to the Pacifier's wound threshold of 4 the first hit counts as 3 and does not meet this wound threshold. The next hit is considered and added to the first to total 6. This exceeds the pacifier's wound threshold so it suffers and wound and all excess is discarded.


The remaining hit is then compared against the next wound's Threshold of 4. Counting as 3 successes: it fails to meet the threshold and does not wound.


  • Move and Shoot: This unit may perform a Move action and Shoot action in the same activation.
  • Anti-Air X: X is added to the hit rolls of all attacks resolved against an Aircraft
  • Attachment (X): This unit may be attached to the unit in parenthesis. An Attachment unit may not perform actions on its activation and may only activate an ability. 
  • Armor X: A vehicle with this rule does not have to reduce its movement or destroy a weapon until it has lost more than X wounds.
  • Cumbersome X: When resolving an attack with this weapon, subtract this number from the dice roll when making a Shoot action or add it when making a Fight action


Unit Types

Units in Cyclopean fall into two major categories
  • Infantry
  • Vehicle
Infantry
Infantry units typically make up the bulk of any force and have the following rules associated with them:
  • Keep Low: Any intervening piece of scatter terrain provides +1 to their wound Threshold against Ranged attacks
  • Effective Fire: Infantry may not perform a Move and a Shoot action in the same activation unless they have Move and Shoot.
  • Boots on the Ground: Only Infantry may hold objectives.
  • Break and Run: If an infantry unit is Engaged, it may perform the Fall Back Double Action on its turn to break engagement. It is no longer engaged and must make a full Move away from the engaging enemy units. Afterwards, roll a D6 and move it that many inches further.

Vehicles

Vehicles operate differently from infantry in a number of ways.
  • Hullbreak: Each time a vehicle loses a wound it must choose to destroy a weapon or halve its movement. 
No Man Left Behind

A unit's movement is always set to that of its slowest member. So if a vehicle has reduced movement, all other vehicles in its unit also have reduced movement as long as that model survives.

  • Mortal Disregard: Vehicle units treat Fall Back as a single action.
  • Stabilizers: A vehicle may Move and Shoot in the same activation.

Unit Subtypes

Many units have a sub-type that influences how they interact with the world. 

Hover

Hover covers a range of things from traditional envelope hovercraft to anti-gravity generators, and also includes things like jump packs or wings. 
  • Hover Units move freely over all terrain types.
  • Hover Vehicles must always choose to reduce their movement when suffering their first wound.

Walker

In Cyclopean there are numerous large mechs and vehicles that rely on their height for advantage. 
  • Walkers may move freely across Water so long as they have not reduced their movement
  • Walkers cannot enter Woods. The dense foliage obscuring optics is a death sentence for any pilot.

Tracked

The myriad of tracked vehicles that make up the bulk of any force. 
  • Tracked units suffer no penalties for movement through any terrain, nor do they gain any bonuses.

Wheeled

The numerous transported, armored cars, and fast response vehicles of the various factions.
  • Wheeled units gain +4" of movement when performing a Move action entirely on a Road.
  • Wheeled units halve their movement in Fields and Woods.

Mechanized

The various suits of power armor, light mechs, and mechanical constructs.
  • Mechanized units are considered infantry for all rules but are affected by Anti-Tank at half-effectiveness (Rounding up).

Attachment

The support units such as medics, wizards, overseers, and priests that accompany armies. 
  • An Attachment may be attached to a Parent unit. If it does so it adopts the Move stat of the parent unit. It will resolve any Actions with the parent unit. It also gains any rules or keywords associated with that parent unit but does not convey its own onto that unit.
  • Attachments maintain a separate activation to activate their abilities. Any ability that applies to "This Unit" also applies to their parent unit as well. 

Aircraft

The jets, helicopters, and flying monstrosities that harry the battlefield.
  • Aircraft must perform at least one Move action during their activation. 
  • Aircraft may not reduce their movement after taking a wound. 
  • Aircraft may not be Charge or be Charged, unless by against or by another Aircraft. 
  • Ranged attacks against an Aircraft are always resolved on the worst result possible: A 6 on a D6, an 8 on a D8, and a 10 on a D10.

Squadron

The various types of complex units made up of many powerful individuals. Be it a sorcerous coven or a lethal kill team, these units are potent fighting forces until the end.
  • Squadrons are infantry units with multiple wound boxes. Unlike regular infantry units, however, these units lose effectiveness with casualties.
  • The weapons profiles for a Squadron unit will show the individual attacks of each member of the squadron. When resolving an attack by this unit, form a dice pool by multiplying the weapons dice by the number of remaining wounds the unit has.
Example

A unit of Deep One warriors is a Squadron. Each undamaged stand has a 2D6 melee attack and 2 wounds. Each stand would contribute 4d6 dice to dice pool. If it were to suffer a wound, it would instead only contribute 2d6 to the dice pool.

Unit Stats

Units in Cyclopean come with a unit card. This is a generally compact reference card that will be used throughout the game. It will tell you what faction a unit belongs to, how many models are in each unit, what elements it generates, and various other combat characteristics.

Name: The name of the unit

Points: The cost in points to include this unit in your army.

Move: A model's speed. Each stand in a unit moves up to this distance when making a Move action.

Fighting: When resolving a Fight action this is the number you are looking to roll equal to or under in order to achieve a hit. A higher number is better.

Stands: The number of models a unit starts the game with. Models and stands can be used interchangeably.

Major Energy: The only, or the leftmost, symbol represents a unit's Major Energy. When drawing from the Energy Deck, this unit may be activated if its corresponding element is drawn.

Minor Energy: Any symbol after the first. These represent excess energy or an overabundance of power that a unit provides. When a unit with a Minor Energy is activated, add a corresponding card to your Energy Pool.

Wound Track: These are a model's wounds, represented by a box with a number in it. When a unit loses a wound, a box is marked off. Once a model loses all of its wounds it is immediately removed as a casualty.

Wound Threshold: The number inside of each Wound Box. This is the number of successful hits needed to check the corresponding wound box.




Weapon Stats

On a unit's card are any weapon it is equipped with. Any weapon with a Range characteristic is considered a Ranged weapon and is used when making a Shoot action. Any weapon with a range of — is a melee weapon and is used when making a Fight action.

Range: The effective firing range of a weapon. This is the distance in inches an enemy stand must be within for a model in this unit to use this weapon. 

Dice/Stand (STD): The number of dice added to the Dice Pool for each unit participating in a Shoot action or Fight action.

Hit: The dice roll required to score a Success when resolving an attack with this weapon. This is sometimes referred to as the 'Hit Number' or 'to hit' number.

To Hit Number

When resolving an attack, Ranged Attacks search for higher numbers. This is represented by a + symbol after the Hit number. 

Melee weapons are search for lower numbers. This is represented by a - symbol after the Hit number.

Rules: This is where any rules that modify the weapon may be included. This includes: Impact, Anti-Tank, Cumbersome, Anti-Air. 

Weapons of Desperation

All units are considered to be equipped with a 2D8 melee weapon with no special rules.

Abilities

Each unit has up to three abilities, each with a different cost to be paid to use. A unit may only activate one ability per activation. These abilities are activated when a unit is selected to be activated but  before any actions are completed. 

An ability will typically be formatted with the ability name and its cost. This ranges from one to three Energy symbols. This is the cost that must be paid to activate an ability. Each ability will describe what the ability does. 

Abilities, unless otherwise specified, have the following restrictions:
  • Duration: An ability only lasts until the end of the turn. If an ability provides a status the unit retains that status until it meets any criteria that would remove that status.
  • Target: A Friendly unit.
  • Stacking: A unit may not benefit from the same ability multiple times regardless of source.
Power at Hand

When a card is drawn from the Energy Deck, it is immediately added to the Energy Pool after selecting a unit to activate. Once a unit is selected, any Minor Energies for that unit are added to the Energy Pool. 

This energy made be used to activate abilities of the selected unit. There is no need to wait.

There are no restrictions on Ordered units spending energy from the Energy Pool when activated, they just don't add their Energy to the pool.

Some abilities state that they can only be used Once per game. This restriction is per unit and does not apply to any other units with the same name.





Energy

Energy is one of the core features of Cyclopean and represents the myriad sources of arcane rites and magics that the forces use. Each faction has its own unique sources of Energy and some even have alternative methods of generating Energy.  In play Energy is typically used to pay for abilities.

Energy Deck: A deck made up of cards drawn from your Extracted units. This deck is used to determine random activations in the Torpor phase.

Energy Pool: The collection of drawn energy cards, minor energies, and alternatively sourced energies. Cards are removed from this pool when spent to pay for abilities. 

Major Energy: The energy type a unit generates in the Extraction phase and the matching card type required to activate it in the Torpor phase.

Minor Energy: The energy type a unit may generate upon activation. This energy is added directly to the Energy Pool upon declaring a unit is activating. 

Each unit card will detail their Major energy and, if they have any, any Minor energies.

When a unit has its energy Extracted in the Extraction phase, it is added to a shuffled deck of cards referred to as the Energy Deck. When it is a player's time to activate, they may draw a card from the top of this deck, adding that card to their Energy Pool, and then selecting a unit with a matching Major Energy type that hasn't activated yet.

Sharing information

The battles in Cyclopean are vile conflicts between different occult orders each steeped in their own arcane rites and lores. They wield secrets that could melt minds and render the weak as dumb-mutes. This game, however, is one meant to played between two normal people for fun. 

During a game, your opponent may wish to see your your units cards and abilities. Always present this information and provide them with it without any reservation. They must know what your units are capable of but they have no right to your machinations and plans. 

Measuring Distances

A player may measure distances at any time during the course of the game. Whether by satellite, augurs, or drones, the battlefield of the future has lost much of its fog of war. 
When measuring the distance from a model, measure from the edge of the model's base to nearest edge of the nearest model in the target unit. A model is within range if any portion of the base is within that distance. 
A model is completely within range when its entire base is within that range. 

When two bases are touching they are considered to be within 0" of each other. This is referred to as "base-to-base."









Line of Sight

Cyclopean uses true Line of Sight: If a model can draw a line to an enemy model then it is considered to be within line of sight. There are some conditions, however, that restrict this. Certain terrain blocks line of sight such as forests. And if a targeted model is majority obscured by a vehicle it is considered to not be in Line of Sight.

Brothers in the Dark

Units may maneuver freely in relation to each other but all models within a unit must remain in cohesion. A unit is considered in cohesion if all models are within 6" of every other model in the same unit.
A unit may not end a Move or Charge action with any models out of Cohesion.

Gameplay

A game of Cyclopean is played in a series of turns. Cyclopean is an alternating activations game. Players select units one at a time, performing at least one action with that unit, before their opponent does the same. They move back and forth until all units have activated. Once the last player has activated their final unit the turn is over.

Turn Structure

A turn has three phases: Extraction, Vigilance, and Torpor.

Extraction Phase

During the Extraction phase, perform the following steps in order:

1: Each player rolls 2d6. The Winner of this roll is Favored and the loser is Scorned. 

2: The Scorned player chooses which of his units are Ordered and which units are to Extracted.

3: The Favored player now does the same.

4: For each Ordered unit, its owner places a token beside the unit designated it as Ordered.

5: For each Extracted unit, its owner adds an Energy to the Energy Deck corresponding to its Major Energy. 

Eldritch Machinations

When resolving priority, ties go to the player that was not previously Favored. If no player was previously favored, such as the first round, roll an additional d6. Continue this until a winner is selected.







Vigilance Phase

During the Vigilance phase, perform the following steps in order:

1: Starting with the Favored player, he may select an Ordered unit to activate. He may also choose to not activate any Ordered units and is considered to have relinquished the phase and may not activate any more units this phase.

2: Once the Favored player has completed his first Activation, the Scorned player may now repeat the process, alternating back and forth until all Ordered units have been activated or relinquished.

3: Players alternate back and forth, choosing at any time to concede the phase. This phase ends once both players have activated all Ordered units or relinquished the phase.

Torpor Phase

During the Torpor phase, perform the following steps in order:

1: Starting with the Favored player, the player may select an Ordered unit or draw a card from his Energy Deck. If he chooses to draw a card from the Energy Deck he must draw one card, adding to his Energy Pool, and select a unit with the same Major Energy as that card to activate. 

2: Once the Favored player has completed his first Activation, the Scorned player may now repeat the process, alternating back and forth until all units have been activated.

3: If a player has no more units to activate but his opponent still does, he may choose to spend an energy from his energy pool to invigorate a unit. By spending a matching Major Energy from his Energy Pool, he may activate a unit that has already activated. This unit may only perform one action this activation and may only be invigorated once per turn.

4: Players empty their Energy Pools, setting aside any Energy Cards added to it.

Activation

Units in Cyclopean can only be activated once per turn, whether it is in the Vigilance Phase or Torpor phase. Depending on the phase what units are eligible to be activated change:

Vigilance Phase: Only Ordered units may be activated in this phase. 

Torpor Phase: Ordered units may be activated at any time a player may choose a unit to activate. An Extracted unit can only be activated by first drawing its corresponding energy from the Energy Deck.

When a unit is Activated it first may activate an ability. This is done by declaring you are activating an ability and paying its ability cost by removing the corresponding Energy cards from your Energy Pool. Once this has been completed, resolve the ability in its entirety before moving on.

After this ability window, a unit may perform two Actions. Unless otherwise noted a unit may perform the same Action multiple times. Any Action performed as a Free does not count against any limits when a unit performs its normal Actions. Some Actions cost both possible Actions, these are referred to as Complex Actions.
Out of Sequence Activations

Some units have special abilities that give them additional activations in a turn. 

If an ability grants a unit an additional activation, treat it as an Ordered unit for the sake of this activation. If it is not Ordered in the Extraction phase, when first activated declare if you are using its free activation or regular activation.

If an ability has a trigger condition for an additional activation, resolve that activation immediately after the activation in which the condition was triggered, ignoring the regular order. This does not count as an activation for the purposes of alternating activations, and allows a player back-to-back activations.

Actions

  • Move: A unit moves each stand up to their Move stat. A unit that performs a Move action may not perform a Shoot action in the same activation.
  • Fight: A unit performs a Melee attack against a unit it is currently Engaged with.
  • Shoot: A unit performs a Ranged Attack against an eligible target.
  • Interact (Simple): A unit interacts with an objective as determined by the scenario.
  • Fall Back (Vehicle): A unit loses the Engaged status and performs a Move action.

Double Actions

  • Charge: This unit selects an enemy model within its Move + 6". This unit performs a Move action, ending as close as possible to enemy unit. (Maneuvering around terrain as needed). Its controlling player then measures the distance between the nearest Friendly model in the unit and the nearest Enemy model and rolls a D6. 
Success

If the roll is equal to or greater than the distance, move each stand a number of inches equal to that roll so that at least one model is base-to-base with the selected unit. They are now engaged and the charging unit immediately performs a Fight.

Failure

If the roll is less than the distance between the two units, the charge has failed and the unit performs no further move. Its activation ends unless it can perform another action.

  • Stalk (Infantry Only, Concealed only): A unit performs a standard Move action and retain its Concealment status. 
  • Interact (Complex): A unit interacts with an objective as determined by the scenario.
  • Fall Back (Infantry): A unit loses the Engaged status and performs a Move action, adding D6 to their Move stat while performing this action.

Ranged Combat

When a unit declares a Shoot action, it is considered to make a Shooting Attack or Ranged Attack, used interchangeably. When making a Shoot Action, perform the following steps:

1: Declare your targets. Infantry units may only declare one target. Vehicles may declare different targets for each weapon the unit is equipped with. All targets must be declared before any dice are rolled.

2: Determine line of sight for each model in the shooting unit. As long as a model can draw line of sight to any model in the target unit it may contribute to the dice pool.

3: Form your dice pool for each weapon. A dice pool is made up of the same weapon from each stand. If a unit has 4 riflemen that each have a 3d6 ranged attack, you would form a dice pool of 12 dice. This may not be combined with another weapon.

4: Roll your dice pool. For each dice that is equal to or greater than your Hit number is considered a Hit. Add your Hits together and compare them to your target's Wound Threshold. If the number of hits meets or exceeds the target's Wound Threshold then it suffers a wound. Remove that many successes from your pool. Continue to compare your hits to the enemy's wound threshold until you may no longer successfully wound them.

Impact and Anti-Infantry/Vehicle

Impact is for heavy armor piercing weapons that are effective on both infantry and vehicles. This reduces the Wound Threshold of each box in the target unit when resolving an attack. 

Anti-Infantry and Anti-Vehicle weapons are specialized, either with flechette rounds or shaped charges their effects are diminished. Each hit by a weapon with Anti-Infantry X counts as X successes. These do not roll over after meeting a Wound Threshold and excess is discarded. Against a target with a Wound Threshold of 3-3, an Anti-Infantry 2 weapon would require 4 successful hits to destroy.

Wounding

A unit with a Ranged Skill of 5+ rolls 6d6 dice when resolving an attack against a unit with a Wound Threshold of 2-2. After discarding the misses, the attacker is left with 3 successes. Combining the results they are able to meet the first wound threshold of 2, causing a wound.  Afterwards, they are left with a single success. It is unable to meet the next Wound Threshold and is discarded, resolving the attack.

A unit may only perform a Fight action against a unit it is Engaged with. Engaged is a condition typically conveyed by being Charged or Charging successfully. Melee in a frantic clash of claws, knives, and fanaticism, with both sides attacking simultaneously. When making a Fight action, perform the following steps:

1: Declare your targets. Each model in a unit may declare any unit they are Engaged with as a target. 


2: For each unit targeted in this way, the defender may then select a target for that unit.


3: Both you and your opponent form dice pools for each unit participating in the Fight. For each targeted unit, form a separate Dice Pool.


4: Each player simultaneously rolls their dice pool. For every roll equal to or less than their Fighting characteristic, the hit is a success. The number of hits are compared to the target's wound threshold, and if meeting or exceeding they cause a wound.


If this Fight action is being resolved as part of a charge, the defending player must remove stands from closest to the enemy, and the attacker replaces that stand with their furthest models as they surge forward into the gap they have created. 




If this Fight action is being resolved as part of a standard activation, casualties are removed from those furthest from the enemy, or 'pulled from the back'.

Terrain

Fields: Fields block Line of Sight for Infantry units. An infantry unit ending a Move action with every model within a field gain Concealment. Walkers may ignore the Concealment status when targeting a unit inside of a Field.

Light Fortifications: Light fortifications are represented by any low linear obstacle. These grant infantry units +1 to their Wound Threshold against any ranged attack drawing Line of Sight over it to at least half their models.

Heavy Fortifications: Buildings, Bunkers and any prepared position. Any unit in a heavy fortification adds +1 to their Wound Threshold against ranged attacks. Additionally, infantry units ending a Move Action in a Heavy Fortification gain Concealment.

Marsh/Mud: Difficult Terrain for Infantry and Wheeled Vehicles.

Roads: Wheeled units that begin a Move Action entirely on a road add 4" to their Move for that action.

Rubble: Difficult Terrain for all vehicles except Hover Vehicles and Tracked Vehicles.

Smoke: Units outside of smoke may not draw line of sight through smoke, but line of sight may be drawn into smoke. Units ending a Move Action in smoke gain Concealment. These are typically represented by a 3" round circle or a 5" round circle. The 3" Smoke marker lasts for 2 turns. A 5" Smoke marker lasts for 3 turns.

Water: Impassable terrain. Walkers without reduced movement, hover units, boats, and amphibious units may move move across it freely. 

Woods/Forest: Heavy woodland copses and bramble tangles. A bane to any elevated fighting platform. Impassable to Hover Units and Walkers. Ending a move with all units within a forest gain Concealment. If this Concealment is lost, it may not be regained without the unit ending a Move Action wholly outside of that forest.

DIfficult Terrain

Difficult Terrain is represented by a myriad of things from anti-tank traps to mud, rubble, and other obstacles that impede effective movement.

Any unit effected by Difficult Terrain reduces their Move to 3".

A unit is effected by Difficult Terrain if it begins its activation with at least one model within Difficult Terrain and does not end a Move action with no models within Difficult Terrain

Hazards

Some terrain is more than just a threat of a broken ankle. Beyond the horrors of war there are many things lurking at the threshold.
When a piece of terrain is described a hazard, a unit that ends a Move action or ends its activation within the Hazard Range must roll a Hazard Check. A typical Hazard will have the number of dice rolled and the result needed to score a successful hit against the unit, resolved as an attack.
A unit can only be forced to make a Hazard Check once per Hazard per turn.
Corruption

Some Hazards cause Corruption. Each time a unit is said to have gained Corruption or performs a Hazard Check that has the Corruption rule, a Corruption marker is placed next to it. For the rest of the game, whenever a unit with Corruption makes a Hazard Check, add that many dice to the Hazard Pool. 

Eldritch Horror

Some beasts and constructs are inured to the twisting sigils, blighting presence, and celestial contempt rained up on them. Whether through some arcane protection, natural stoic, or even the sources of corruption, these things are immune to the taint.

Eldritch: A unit with the Eldritch rule suffers no penalties for any Corruption markers it may have and ignores Eldritch Hazards. It may still benefit from any bonuses conferred by Eldritch Objectives.

Eldritch Objectives

Obelisk: An obelisk has a random Energy determined at the start of the game by players rolling off and then drawing an Energy Card. During the Extraction phase, a player with at least one unit within 6" may add an Energy of the Obelisk's type to their Energy Pool. If they do so, they must add a Corruption a friendly unit within 6" of the Obelisk.

Hanging Tree: A unit within 3" of the Hanging Tree may not be Extracted and must be Ordered.

Font of Madness: If a player controls this objective and it is their turn to activate a unit, they may spend an Energy to draw two Energy Cards, selecting one to activate and shuffling the other into the Energy Deck.

Twisting Sigil: If a player controls this objective, during the Extraction phase, a player may change the Major Energy of a unit within 3" until the end of the turn. This is done before resolving step 2 of the Extraction Phase.

Local Seer: A unit beginning its activation within 3" of this objective ignores Concealment for this activation. Only one unit per turn may benefit from this objective.

Blood Altar: Before Step 2 of the Extraction Phase, a player with a unit within 3" of this Objective may remove a stand from that unit to add 2 Energy of any kind to their Energy Pool. This can only happen once per player per turn no matter the number of Blood Altars.









Eldritch Hazards

Deep Field Acoustic Survey Technical Memorandum
AZ-TM-206577UNLIMITED DISTRIBUTION
Abstract. A persistent non-thermal signal has been recovered from four independent arrays at a bearing that does not resolve to any catalogued source. The signal is periodic, the period is not constant, and the variation in the period is itself periodic. This memorandum documents the instrumentation, the reduction, and the reasons the programme's original hypothesis has been withdrawn.

1. Introduction

The survey was commissioned to characterise background noise in the 0.1–4 Hz band. It has not done so. What follows is an account of the anomaly and of the four independent attempts to attribute it to instrumentation.

2. Instrumentation

Four arrays, geographically separated, differing in manufacture, calibration standard, and burial depth. The signal appears in all four with a phase relationship consistent with a single distant source and inconsistent with every terrestrial explanation the programme has been able to construct.

3. Discussion

The programme's original hypothesis attributed the signal to a rotating source. This has been withdrawn. A rotating source does not account for the harmonic content, and it does not account for the fact that the harmonic content is musical in a sense the programme has been unable to render as a figure.

3.1 On the term used in the field

Personnel at three of the four arrays independently described the signal as piping. This term does not appear in the instrumentation record and was not suggested by the programme. It is recorded here because it was arrived at independently, which is the only property of it we can measure.

4. Conclusions

4.1 The signal is real.
4.2 The signal is not local.
4.3 The period is lengthening, and the rate at which it lengthens is lengthening.
4.4 The programme recommends no further increase in array sensitivity.

Recommendation withdrawn

Conclusion 4.4 was entered by the principal investigator and countersigned. The principal investigator has since requested that it be recorded as the only recommendation he has ever been certain of.

Sealed

Extrapolating the drift backward gives a period of zero at a date the programme has calculated four times and reported once. Extrapolating it forward gives a period equal to the age of the instrumentation within the working lifetime of everyone named on this document. The signal is not a source. It is something turning over in its sleep, and the piping is not the sound of the sleeper.
